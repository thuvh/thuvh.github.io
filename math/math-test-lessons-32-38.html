🎲 Reroll and reprint — this sheet lasts the whole block

Reroll gives every part fresh numbers, names, colours and pattern units, and the answer page recalculates itself. Ranges stay inside the lessons: ten-frames and graphs within 10, pattern units are AB · AAB · ABB · ABC only — never a growing pattern, which this block has not taught.

The one thing this sheet measures that asking cannot

Parts E and F make the child write the repeating part before writing what comes next. A child who reads only the last shape and alternates can finish a pattern correctly without ever finding the unit — and a sheet that asks only for the next shape marks that as right. An empty “part that repeats” box with a correct next shape is the error worth catching; it is on the answer page.

The two hardest questions, on purpose

A, last question: a named child has n counters and needs 10 — the frame is already part-filled, so the missing counters have to be drawn, not counted up in the head. C, last question: two steps — total all three rows, then add the extra votes. An answer equal to the plain total means the second step was dropped.

Two spoken questions — ask after marking, never before

1. For parts E, F and G: “Show me the part that repeats, and say it out loud three times.” — a child who has the unit says it as one chunk (). A child who reads piece by piece off the page is reading, not predicting, and that is the whole lesson of 36.

2. For part C: “Did anyone lose a vote?” — the right answer is no. Comparing two rows takes nothing away from either row; how many more is a difference, not a subtraction story. Getting the arithmetic right does not separate the two, which is why this gets asked.

Nothing on the child’s pages carries a mark scheme, and this page carries none either. What goes in the log is which part was wrong and what the child said while working, with the date — not a count of correct answers. The same part wrong three times running is the signal.
