Test 5 — What is the main idea of the story?

Language Arts · Wonders Grade 1, U2 Weeks 1 + 2. Focus: review of e + u · nouns · -ed · ’s.

Printing: Ctrl/Cmd+P → A4, single-sided, scale 100%, untick “headers and footers”.

4 pages: 2 for the child (Part 1 · Parts 2–5) + 2 for the grown-up (Part 3 script, answer key, reading the results) — print separately, do not show the child.

Structure: 20 questions, 5 parts — per example-la-structure-weekly-v2.md

  • Part 1 — short story + 6 comprehension questions (question 6 is written, 2 points)
  • Part 2 — 4 high-frequency words to fill into a blank
  • Part 3 — 3 phonemic awareness items: the grown-up says it, the child listens, no reading
  • Part 4 — 4 phonics items
  • Part 5 — 3 grammar and structural analysis items

The story is a retelling, not a transcript

The content comes from “Farmer's Story – Life on the Happy Farm | Bat and Friends” (Little Fox) — Ben looks after the animals, Bat and his friends play together. But the words in the video (hammock, hula hoop, contest) sit far outside the sounds the school has taught, so the story was rewritten using only Unit 2 Week 1–2 sounds. The farmer is named Ben — the same name as Week 1's shared read “Good Job, Ben!”, and Ben is a short e word.

🔴 The decodability boundary is machine-checked

Every word in the story, the fill-in sentences, the phonics and PA choices and the Part 5 material sits inside: the 30 Start Smart words + the Unit 1 spelling list + short e (e, ea) + short u + the 10 high-frequency words of Unit 2 Weeks 1–2. There are no digraphs (sh th ch wh ph ng), no magic e, no long vowels, no r-controlled vowels and no final consonant clusters. Run check_boundary.py to verify.

The comprehension questions are deliberately NOT inside the boundary — the grown-up reads them aloud, exactly as on a real Wonders test.

Pacing the 5 tests

One test per sitting, 12–15 minutes. Do not do two in one day. Order 1 → 2 → 3 → 4 → 5: tests 1 and 3 are short e (Week 1), tests 2 and 4 are short u (Week 2), and test 5 is a mixed review and the only one that asks for the main idea.

TEST 5 · READING THE RESULTSfor the grown-up

🔴 Do not add up a total and then feel pleased or worried. This test returns a week to go back to, not a score. Read it part by part — the thresholds below are McGraw Hill's own 75%-per-section rule.

Threshold per part, and where to go back to if it fails

PartPassWhat to do if it fails
1 · Reading (1–6)≥ 5/7Read the story out loud together, then ask again. If the child reads it fluently and still answers wrong, the problem is comprehension, not decoding. Do not blame phonics.
2 · High-Frequency Words (7–10)≥ 3/4Put the week's five words on cards, one minute each evening: again · help · new · there · use (Week 1) or could · live · one · then · three (Week 2). These are sight words — they cannot be sounded out, so do not make the child try.
3 · Phonemic Awareness (11–13)≥ 2/3🔴 Fix this by ear, not on paper. Play it in the car: you say a word in separate sounds, the child blends it; then swap roles. Three minutes a day. Printing more worksheets fixes the wrong thing.
4 · Phonics (14–17)≥ 3/4Check which sound was missed (the “What it measures” column on the answer sheet): missing e/ea items → go back to Unit 2 Week 1; missing u items → Week 2. Missing both → stop giving new tests and re-read those two weeks' decodables.
5 · Grammar and SA (18–20)≥ 2/3Missed the -ed item → Unit 2 Week 1. Missed the ’s item or the commas → Week 2. Grammar is not measured on the school's weekly test — so this is the only place it shows up.

Three easy ways to mark this wrong

🔴 Question 6 scores the idea, not the spelling. “the pets r fed” still earns the full 2 points. Marking spelling here measures two things at once.

⚠️ Part 1 is not limited to the sounds taught so far. The comprehension questions are read aloud by the grown-up — exactly as on a real Wonders test. If the child stumbles on the printed question, that is not a phonics error.

✅ The story is the opposite — it is locked inside the boundary. Every word in the story, the fill-in sentences and the four phonics items sits inside what the school has taught through Unit 2 Week 2. So a stumble inside the story is a real signal, worth writing down.
